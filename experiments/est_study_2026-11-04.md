# Range-count estimator study on real SIFT (100k), 100 queries x 5 radii

Setup: SIFT 100k, IVF nlist=256 (kmeans on 40k), radii = pooled-distance quantiles
[0.005, 0.02, 0.05, 0.2, 0.5] → mean counts [783, 1941, 3823, 13575, 47109].
Cost = distance evaluations. Ground truth = exact scan. Host: `joysuch`, CPU only.

| estimator | mean rel. err ↓ | p95 ↓ | cost ↓ |
|---|---|---|---|
| uniform sampling S=4,000 | 0.1760 | 1.000 | 400,000 |
| uniform sampling S=12,000 | 0.0908 | 0.414 | 1,200,000 |
| uniform sampling S=20,000 | 0.0719 | 0.316 | 2,000,000 |
| **two-phase k=16 lists, S=4,000** | **0.0329** | **0.110** | **1,069,300** |
| **two-phase k=32 lists, S=4,000** | **0.0151** | **0.0497** | **1,682,904** |
| **two-phase k=64 lists, S=6,000** | **0.0036** | **0.0148** | **3,075,590** |
| certified-list importance sampling (2000+1000) | 0.2168 | 1.000 | 1,245,241 |
| certified-list importance sampling (4000+2000) | 0.1579 | 1.000 | 2,426,229 |

## Reading

1. **Two-phase beats uniform sampling at *lower* cost on real data.** At 1.07M evaluations the
   two-phase estimator reaches 0.033 mean relative error where uniform sampling needs 1.2M for
   0.091 — 2.8x more accurate for less work. At 1.68M it reaches 0.015, versus 0.072 for uniform
   sampling at 2.0M (4.8x better).
2. **The gain is specific to clustered data.** The same comparison on synthetic uniform vectors
   (no clusters) showed no advantage at equal cost: without clusters the near lists do not
   concentrate the hits. This is why the estimator is validated on real SIFT.
3. **Certified-list importance sampling was rejected.** Using the certified ε-graph list
   predicate as an importance-sampling frame over-covers (many lists qualify), so reweighting
   variance dominates: 0.16–0.22, worse than plain sampling at comparable cost.
4. Consequence for VDM-Bench: the `ana-vec` submission now uses two-phase (k=32, S=4000) and the
   old purely-sampled variant stays on the leaderboard as `ana-vec-sampling`, so the improvement
   is visible as an ablation on the same task.
